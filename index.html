Art Upcycling

Art Upcycling: a creative process that transforms the perception, desirability, and potential value of pre-loved garments through different artistic techniques.

Restore desirability

Transform a pre-loved garment into a distinctive, art-infused piece that people want to wear, value and keep.

Re-enter the fashion cycle

Give existing garments new relevance, creating opportunities for designers and brands to incorporate them into new or updated collections, resell them, or reach new audiences.

Personal Sustainability

Creating art on garments is also a personal, healing process, allowing me to transform emotions and experiences into colours, textures and meaningful expressions.

Environmental value & Impact

The potential to reduce waste and avoid emissions associated with new production, especially when the transformed garment genuinely replaces the purchase of a new item.

Artistic Techniques

Pantha Rhei explores a range of artistic textile techniques, including natural dyeing, freehand acrylic painting, mechanical ecoprinting and screen printing, transforming pre-loved garments into unique pieces of wearable art.

Decarbonization of Fashion

The CO₂ emission factor vision aims to accelerate fashion decarbonization by making environmental impact measurable, transparent, and actionable across the value chain.

Business to business: Advocate for Regenerative Fashion

Pantharhei’s B2B model aims to build partnerships with fashion brands, retailers, small restaurants, interior design actors, creative businesses, and organizations seeking to transform textile waste into meaningful creative and social value.

Creative collaborations

  • For fashion brands: co-create capsule collections through art upcycling
  • For small restaurants: regenerate placemats and napkins that would otherwise become waste due to use and stains
  • Regenerate interior design pieces such as sofas, curtains, cushions, poufs, carpets, etc., that would otherwise become waste due to use and stains

Circular materials partnerships

  • suitable surplus textiles.
  • Discarded garments.
  • Returned stock and unsold stock
